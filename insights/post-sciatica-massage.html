Does massage actually help sciatica, or does it just feel that way?

September 2, 2026

The first time a massage therapist worked on my lower back, I remember thinking, this is it, I'm fixed. The pain that had been shooting down my leg for weeks finally let go, just for an hour or two. I booked another session for the following week, certain I'd found the answer.

I hadn't, not entirely. But it took me longer than I'd like to admit to understand why.

What sciatica actually is

Sciatica isn't its own condition, it's a symptom. It shows up when the sciatic nerve, the longest nerve in the body, gets compressed or irritated somewhere along its path from the lower spine down through the hip, buttock, and leg. In my case it was a mix of a mildly bulging disc and a very tight piriformis muscle doing its best to guard an injury that had mostly healed. The result was the same regardless of the cause: a burning, electric kind of pain that made sitting through a meeting feel like an endurance sport.

Where massage genuinely helped

To be fair to massage, it did real things for me, not just placebo comfort. Loosening the muscles around my lower back and hips took some of the mechanical pressure off the nerve directly. My circulation improved enough that the whole area felt less swollen and reactive. And there's something to be said for the nervous system simply calming down when a session ends, the pain volume seemed to drop a few notches even after I'd left the table.

For a few days after each session, I moved better. I slept better. I believed the pain was finally behind me.

Why the relief never lasted

Then, without fail, it crept back. Usually around day four or five, right as I'd start feeling like myself again, the tightness would return, and with it the pain.

What I eventually understood is that massage was treating the muscles reacting to the problem, not the problem itself. The disc was still doing what it was doing. The nerve was still getting irritated in the same spot. My muscles would tense right back up to protect it, and I'd be back where I started, just with a nicer hour behind me. This overview of sciatica causes and treatment explains that gap between symptom relief and addressing the source better than I could at the time.

What actually changed things

The turning point wasn't finding a better massage therapist, it was finally seeing a sciatica chiropractor in Surbiton who looked at the spine and the nerve pathway itself, not just the muscles around them. Once I understood exactly what was compressing the nerve, treatment stopped being guesswork.

From there, a combination of things made the difference:

Targeted spinal work to take direct pressure off the nerve. Specific stretches for my piriformis and hamstrings, done consistently rather than only when I remembered. And, yes, massage, but now as one part of the plan instead of the whole plan, used to manage the muscle tension between the sessions actually addressing the cause.

What I'd tell someone just starting out

If massage is giving you a few good days here and there, that's not nothing, and I wouldn't tell you to stop. But if the relief keeps fading and the pain keeps returning to the same spot, that's usually a sign the underlying cause hasn't been addressed yet. It's worth getting a real assessment rather than cycling through massage appointments indefinitely, hoping the next one sticks.

It took me longer than it needed to, mostly because the short-term relief was so convincing. I'm hoping this saves someone else a few months of that same loop.
